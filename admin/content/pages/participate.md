## A working session, not a talk {#working-sessions}

A working session has a topic, an owner and an outcome. The owner brings a question, a draft, a problem or a project; the people in the room work on it with them; and the session ends with something written: a decision, a draft, a list of next steps, a pull request. Talks happen, but they are the start of a conversation, not the whole of it.

- **A topic.** One question or one piece of work, small enough for the time.
- **An owner.** The person who proposed it, who keeps it moving afterwards.
- **Participants.** Anyone who wants to help, from any side: developers, security people, CISOs, students, vendors, researchers.
- **An outcome.** Written in the open, linked from the session's page.

## Every participant is a presenter {#presenters}

At the in-person summits the schedule was built by the participants: anyone could propose a session, and sessions were added during the week. That is what made them work, and it is what this version keeps. If you take part, you will have a page here, with the sessions you ran or joined, what you said, and what came out of it.

## What is new in this version {#new}

The summits never had the capacity to follow up properly. The recordings, the notes and the people were there; the time to connect them was not. This version is built with agents doing that work, under a person's responsibility:

- **A page for every participant**, built from what they did at the summit: sessions, videos, notes, projects. Yours to correct.
- **Sessions mapped and linked.** The recordings and pages of the past summits are being read, so that a session leads to the people, the topics and the projects around it.
- **Follow-ups that happen.** After a session, its outcome and next steps go to the people in it, and the open items are kept visible.
- **A personalised path.** What to join next, based on what you worked on before, not a single schedule for everyone.

None of these is running yet; they are the first things this site will build. See [what runs today](/about/#status).

## How to take part {#how}

1. **Propose a session.** A topic you want to work on with others, for a remote session or for the London events. Open an issue or a pull request on [this site's repository](https://github.com/SGit-AI/SGit-AI__Website__Open-Security-Summit), with a title, a paragraph, and the outcome you want.
2. **Join one.** The [events page](/events/) lists what is planned.
3. **Bring a project.** If you lead an open project that wants an independent home, read [hosted projects](/projects/).
4. **Help fund it.** The summit runs on time and on sponsorship that buys no content. See [fund the summit](/fund/).
