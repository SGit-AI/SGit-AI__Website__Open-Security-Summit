## The first sponsors {#sponsors}

!block sponsors

Disclosure: Dinis Cruz, who directs Open Security Summit CIC, is involved in all three. That is why they are first, and it is why the rule below matters: they get no more say in the sessions than anyone else. More sponsors, from outside, are what the summit needs next.

## What it pays for {#what}

- **The working sessions.** Running, recording and following up each one. See [the next phase](/articles/the-next-phase-ad-hoc-sessions.html).
- **The London events.** A venue, the days, the recording, and travel for the people who could not otherwise come.
- **The archive.** Reading eight years of sessions and recordings into pages people can use.
- **The hosted projects.** The legal and administrative work of hosting them properly.

## The rule {#rule}

Sponsors do not buy sessions, speaking slots or content. That was true at every in-person summit, and it is what made the room trustworthy. A sponsor is named and thanked, and its people take part on the same terms as everyone else.

## Sponsor a session or a series {#sessions}

The cheapest way to start is one session, or a series on one subject. A sponsor of a session is named on its page and in its follow-up, and does not choose who speaks or what the outcome says.
