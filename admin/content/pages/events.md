## The formats {#formats}

### Working sessions, called when the participants can make them {#remote}

From 2020 the summit ran online, first as a virtual week and then as mini-summit weeks fitted into a fixed grid. The new sessions drop the grid: each one is called when the people who need to be in it can make it, on a subject that matters now. They keep what worked online, the reach and the recordings, and add what was missing: a page before, the outcome written down, and the follow-up sent to the people in the session. Why: [the next phase](/articles/the-next-phase-ad-hoc-sessions.html).

### In person, in London {#london}

The in-person summits were the best of what the Open Security Summit did: a room of people who came to work, not to listen. The London events start smaller than the five-day weeks of 2017 to 2019, and keep the same rule. Every session is a working session, and every participant is expected to contribute.

### Sessions for hosted projects {#project-sessions}

The [hosted projects](/projects/) will use the summit's sessions to work in the open: reviews of drafts, decisions that need more than one company in the room, and onboarding of new contributors.
