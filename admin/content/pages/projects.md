## Why the summit {#why}

The Open Security Summit has always been about developers and security people working together, and it has been a UK community interest company since 2019. A project hosted here gets a legal and administrative host, a place to run working sessions in the open, and the summit's community, without a new foundation having to be set up for it.

## What hosting means {#hosting}

- **The CIC is the host.** It holds contracts, finances and accountability.
- **The programme sets common rules.** How a project is admitted, the governance templates, and what happens if a project moves on or closes.
- **The project runs itself.** Its own charter, steering group and maintainers decide its technical direction.
- **Sponsorship buys no control.** Funding gives no veto, and the same rule held at every summit: sponsors did not get content.

## Hosting is not independence {#independence}

Dinis Cruz directs the CIC, and is also a founder of some of the companies whose work may be hosted here. So hosting a project separates it from a company as an organisation, but it does not, by itself, make its governance independent. That has to be designed and written down for each project: a public charter, and a steering group with a majority independent of the company that started it. Until that is in place, a hosted project is not described as independently governed.

## Bringing a project {#apply}

A good fit is an open project, under an open licence, that is useful to more than one company and wants a neutral place to be governed and worked on. Open an issue on [this site's repository](https://github.com/SGit-AI/SGit-AI__Website__Open-Security-Summit) with what the project is, its licence, who maintains it, and why it wants a home. The admission rules will be published here once the first project has been through them.
