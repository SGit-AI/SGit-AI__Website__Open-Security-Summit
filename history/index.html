History

From a villa in the Algarve to a restart in London.

The Open Security Summit grew out of the OWASP summits, where the working-session format was invented. It has always had one idea: the people who come do the work.

  1. Nov 2008

    OWASP European Summit

    Algarve, Portugal · in person

    About 80 OWASP leaders. Two days of tracks and project workshops, then two days of open working sessions to set the 2009 agenda. Where the working-session format starts.

  2. Feb 2011

    OWASP Global Summit

    Lisbon, Portugal · in person

    About 175 people from 20+ countries, four days, "designed in a working session style format": parallel working tracks, no vendor booths, no lecture-only talks.

  3. 12–16 Jun 2017

    OWASP Summit 2017

    Woburn Forest Center Parcs, near London · in person

    Five days, 16 tracks, over 170 working sessions and 200+ participants, in person and remote, in shared villas. Its best-known outcome is the Mobile Security Testing Guide. History of the summits

  4. 4–8 Jun 2018

    Open Security Summit 2018

    Woburn Forest Center Parcs, England · in person

    The first Open Security Summit: the 2017 format, broadened from OWASP to security in general. Keynotes from Adam Shostack, Simon Wardley, Jane Frankland and others, and a one-day summit on 3 July. 2018 site

    • About 300 sessions on the site, 129 of them working sessions
    • 143 participants on site, 69 remote
    • 194 outcome pages
  5. 3–7 Jun 2019

    Open Security Summit 2019

    Woburn Forest Center Parcs, England · in person

    The second week at Woburn, with keynotes on SAMM v2, the MSTG, Juice Shop, ZAP, Cynefin and Wardley maps. The year Open Security Summit CIC was incorporated, on 9 April 2019. 2019 site

    • 150 sessions, 123 of them working sessions
    • 130 participants on site, 28 remote
    • Biggest tracks: OWASP SAMM (28), OWASP MSTG (22)
  6. Jun 2020

    Open Security Summit 2020

    Fully online

    The pandemic moved the summit online: two training weeks from 1 June, then the summit week of 15–19 June, with new tracks on Covid apps, Cynefin and Team Topologies. 2020 site

    • Summit week: 60 sessions, 516 attendees, 62 speakers
    • Training weeks: 50 sessions, 722 attendees
  7. Oct 2020 – Dec 2024

    The mini-summits

    Online · free to attend

    Online summit weeks every one to two months, 27 of them: threat modelling, DevSecOps, governance, Wardley maps, and from 2023 some of the first sessions on generative AI and agents for security teams. open-security-summit.org

    • About 340 session pages from Oct 2020 to Dec 2024
    • 219 recorded videos linked from the site
    • 241 people organised sessions
  8. 2025

    The pause

    A March 2025 mini-summit was announced and never ran. The website had grown too complex to change quickly, and there was nobody to follow up. Why.

  9. 2026

    The restart

    Online, then London in person

    A regular run of short working sessions, called when the participants can make them, starting with php2shell; in-person events in London; a programme of hosted open projects starting with Agent Behaviour Policies; and this site. What is next.

What stayed the same

From 2008 to 2024 the format changed (villas, then Zoom) and the rule did not. In the words of every year's README: "This is not a conference with uni-directional presentations, this is a working summit with working sessions." The schedule was "led by you": anyone could create a session and add it. Sessions had organisers, not speakers, and everyone was a participant. Sponsors paid for the week, never for the content.

Where these facts come from

The repositories of the summit's websites, read on 21 October 2026: oss2018, oss2019, oss2020 and oss2021, the last of which is open-security-summit.org. Session and participant counts are pages in those repositories, which include some placeholders and cancelled sessions, so they are approximate. The 2008, 2011 and 2017 summits are from the history of the OWASP in-person summits on diniscruz.ai. The 2020 attendance figures are the ones on open-security-summit.org's home page.
